Section 2

How to Submit a Great SE Request

Requests are submitted via Salesforce. The quality of your request directly determines how fast SE can engage and how well-prepared they'll be.

Weak requests get sent back. Requests missing context, objectives, or stakeholder info will be paused — SE will ask you to clarify before proceeding. Low-quality repeat submissions are escalated to sales managers.
✅ What Every Strong Request Must Have
  • ✓
    Situation / Why Change — What's prompting this prospect to evaluate now? What's the trigger?
  • ✓
    Specific Pain — Not "improve engagement." What is actually broken or missing today?
  • ✓
    Desired Business Outcome — Measurable if possible (e.g., improve mobile engagement by 15%, reduce churn by X%)
  • ✓
    Buying Position — Build vs Buy / New to Messaging / Rip & Replace / OneSignal Expansion
  • ✓
    Current Tech Stack — What messaging tools are they on? Any known pain with those?
  • ✓
    Meeting Objective — What should the customer decide or understand by the end of this session?
  • ✓
    Stakeholder Mapping — Who attends? What are their titles and decision-making roles?
  • ✓
    Meeting Type — See types below. Picking the right type helps SE prepare the right way.

Meeting Types — Pick the Right One

Technical Discovery ▶
Understand the customer's messaging workflows, data model, and technical environment. Use when discovery is done but needs technical depth — unclear event/data structure, multiple systems, complex lifecycle needs. SE Effort: Medium.
Standard Demo ▶
Show core capabilities and general fit. Best for early-stage deals where the customer is still exploring. SE will cover user profile management, segmentation, channels, and lifecycle messaging concepts. SE Effort: Low.
Custom Demo ▶
Specific messaging workflows are defined — SE tailors the demo to the customer's actual use case, KPIs, and value pillars. Requires more prep. Requires strong context in the request. SE Effort: Medium–High.
Technical Discussion / Q&A ▶
Validate architecture, integrations, and technical feasibility. Ideal after an initial demo when the customer asks "how would this work in our system?" SE walks through data ingestion, event flow, identity model, and integration patterns. SE Effort: Medium.
Technical Air Cover ▶
AE leads the call, SE is present to answer targeted technical questions and reinforce credibility. Best for Commercial deals or situations where SE credibility is needed without deep solutioning. SE Effort: Low.
POC / Trial Scoping ▶
SE defines evaluation type, success criteria, scope, and timeline. Trials = early-stage, self-serve validation. POCs = structured, SE-led, higher effort. Always involve SE before committing to an evaluation — gray-area decisions should be routed through SE. SE Effort: High.
Upsell Support ▶
Support expansion for existing customers. SE understands current usage, identifies gaps and expansion opportunities, and prepares tailored recommendations. SE Effort: Low–Medium.
What happens after you submit: SE reviews within 24 hours, claims the request, and tags you to confirm ownership and begin prep alignment. For Top Deals, SE engagement is proactive — no waiting required.
Section 3

What SE Actually Does

SE's job isn't to "run demos." It's to reduce technical uncertainty at critical moments in the buying journey — so deals move forward with confidence.

On Every Engagement
  • Assess Technical Win Readiness before accepting the request
  • Sync with AE 12–48 hrs before to align on agenda and objectives
  • Execute the engagement with a defined goal, not just a walkthrough
  • Track where the deal sits across the 5 Technical Win signals
  • Share post-engagement feedback, action items, and recommended next steps with the AE
On Top / High-Touch Deals
  • Own the Technical Win strategy end-to-end
  • Lead discovery, demo design, and validation
  • Proactively engage and align technical stakeholders
  • Identify and resolve technical risks early
  • Scope and execute POCs with defined success criteria
  • Weekly 15-min AE–SE sync throughout the sales cycle

After Every Session — What to Expect from SE

Step 1
Technical Win Status Update
SE shares where the deal stands across the 5 technical win signals — what's validated, what's open, what's at risk.
Step 2
Action Items & Recommended Next Steps
SE documents follow-ups, customer commitments, and what should happen next (technical discussion, POC, architecture review, etc.).
Step 3
Feedback to AE on Readiness & Risks
SE shares observations on deal readiness, stakeholder gaps, technical risks surfaced, and anything the AE needs to follow up on.
Step 4
SE Notes Updated in Salesforce
SE logs the engagement with date, outcome, and progression notes — this is the record used in pipeline reviews and inspection.
SE engagement is not defined by activity. We don't measure success by the number of demos run. We measure it by whether we advanced the Technical Win — did the customer move from uncertainty to confidence?
Section 4

The Technical Win Framework

A Technical Win isn't a single moment — it's built progressively across the deal. Use this tracker to understand where your deal stands.

The shift: We used to ask "Did we run a demo?" Now we ask "Did we achieve validation?" A deal should not progress through stages unless the required Technical Win signals are met.

The 5 Technical Win Signals — Click to mark as validated

SIGNAL 01
Use Case Validation
Customer understands how they'll execute their messaging workflows in OneSignal end-to-end
✓ Customer describes the workflow themselves
SIGNAL 02
Architecture Fit
Data flow exists or is verbally confirmed — OneSignal fits within the customer's tech stack
✓ Data flow diagram exists or verbally confirmed
SIGNAL 03
Stakeholder Alignment
Engineering and Product agree "this works" — not just the commercial champion
✓ Eng + Product agree "this works"
SIGNAL 04
Risk Resolution
No open technical blockers — integration, migration, security, ownership risks surfaced and addressed
✓ No open blockers including in Slack/email threads
SIGNAL 05 — REQUIRED
Decision Confidence
Customer explicitly confirms the solution will work. Conversation shifts from "can it work?" to "how do we roll this out?"
✓ Customer discussing rollout and next steps
Technical Win Progress 0 / 5 signals

The Four Stages

Stage 1
Technical Win Readiness (Prepare)
Is the deal ready for SE engagement? Required before SE accepts a request:
  • Clear "why now" — trigger and urgency identified
  • Defined messaging use case
  • Understanding of root cause
  • High-level customer environment known (tools, data, channels)
  • Stakeholders identified, meeting objective defined
Stage 2
Validation (Build)
Prove the solution works in the customer's environment:
  • Messaging use case can be executed end-to-end
  • OneSignal fits within customer architecture and data flow
  • Messaging can be triggered and orchestrated as required
  • Solution is feasible in their specific environment
Stage 3
Commitment (Secure)
All signals aligned, deal ready to move:
  • All 5 Technical Win signals present
  • Stakeholders aligned on feasibility and ownership
  • Minimal open technical questions
  • Clear agreement on next steps (POC, rollout, or commercial)
Stage 4
Technical Win (Support Close)
Remove all remaining risk, confirm full confidence:
  • Implementation approach confirmed
  • Ownership validated (who does what)
  • All risks resolved, stakeholders aligned
  • Customer has decision confidence — ready to close
Section 5

The SE Coverage Model

SE is high-impact and capacity-constrained. Not all deals get the same level of support — here's how coverage works and what drives prioritization.

🎯 High-Touch (Top Deal Model)
For your 1–2 most important deals, jointly identified with SE management.
  • SE is proactive, pre-aligned, and co-owns the Technical Win
  • Weekly 15-min AE–SE sync (strategy, tech win path, risks)
  • Continuous engagement from Stage 1 through close
  • SE capacity: ~10–12 high-touch deals per SE per quarter
Primarily: Strategic + select Mid-Market based on deal profile
📋 Scaled / On-Demand
For the rest of your pipeline — request-driven support at key moments.
  • Submit via Salesforce; SE claims within 24 hours
  • Targeted support: demos, technical Q&A, validation
  • AE owns deal progression; SE provides targeted assist
  • Async options: #ask-solutions-engineering, Office Hours
Primarily: Mid-Market, Commercial, non-priority Strategic
